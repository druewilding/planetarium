# Planetarium glossary

- **Earth sky:** The sky as seen from a selected location on Earth's surface, at a selected instant.
- **Globe view:** The view of Earth from above its surface, where the lit and dark sides can be seen together.
- **Guided lift-off:** The transition from the selected Earth surface location into the globe view without resetting the simulation clock.
- **Apparent size:** How much of the sky an object spans from the observer's location, regardless of its physical diameter.
- **Simulation clock:** The instant shown by all views, which can differ from the current real-world time.
- **Observer local time:** The civil date and time at the selected Earth location, not necessarily the date and time where the visitor is physically located.
- **Daylight dimmer:** A viewing aid that reveals objects above the horizon by reducing daylight glare without changing the simulated instant or the illumination of celestial bodies.
- **Observer location:** The point on Earth's surface from which the Earth sky is seen, independent of the visitor's physical location.
- **Orbit overview:** A not-to-scale view of the Moon's motion around Earth that makes both bodies easy to see.
- **Cheese scene:** The fictional first-person interior of a wheel of cheese shown when no observer location has been selected.
- **Demo sky:** The Earth sky at a clearly labeled example location that a visitor explicitly chooses without sharing their own location.
